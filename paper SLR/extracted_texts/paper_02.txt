ABSTRACT
Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) are digital learning environments
employing Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the form of knowledge trac-
ing (KT) to craft personalized learning plans for students. Learning
the basics of programming language directs the students to write
instructions to perform tasks. With this, educational tools like
Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) have been developed to aid edu-
cators and students. This study introduces the DAS3H model and
Case-based Reasoning to assist students in mastering programming
language by accurately identifying learners’ difficulties and Mod-
eling Student Learning and Forgetting for Optimally Scheduling
Distributed Practice Skills. Through rigorous literature analysis,
this paper aims to propose an Intelligent Programming Tutor (IPT)
to improve the efficiency of studying introductory programming
courses. Additionally, the paper outlines several knowledge-tracing
algorithms and models, along with feedback tools. The critical anal-
ysis of previous studies helped to understand the flexible approach
to learning materials, including spaced repetition, digital flash-
cards, and diverse study modes that cater to individual learning
preferences. Additionally, including Case-Based Reasoning (CBR)
in the expert model improves programming skills and enhances
problem-solving abilities.

1 INTRODUCTION
The evolution of technology over the decades has led to a growing
demand for individuals possessing technical skills. Educational
institutions globally have adapted their curricula to impart knowl-
edge and expertise in STEM subjects, with a particular focus on
computer programming [1]. Since mastering the fundamentals of
programming is pivotal, the Introduction to Computer Program-
ming course plays a critical role in shaping students’ abilities and
ensuring their success in an ever-evolving technological landscape
[2]. To this end, educational tools like Intelligent Tutoring Systems
(ITS) have been developed to aid both educators and students.
Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) are digital learning environ-
ments employing Artificial Intelligence (AI) in the form of knowl-
edge tracing (KT) to craft personalized learning plans for students
[3][4]. These systems aim to mimic human tutoring in digital set-
tings, supporting students as they acquire knowledge and skills in
various fields, including computer programming [ 5]. In the context
of this paper, we will use ”Intelligent Programming Tutors (IPTs)”
instead of ”Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITSs)” since IPTs are ITSs
that are focused on programming, but essentially has the same struc-
ture [6][7]. However, despite the availability of numerous methods
and tools designed to assist programming students, there remains
a substantial dropout rate in introductory programming courses
[8]. Consequently, further enhancements in IPTs are essential to
bolster the learning experience.
Intelligent Programming Tutors (IPTs) typically consist of four
primary components: a student model, a knowledge domain model,
an expert model, and a user interface model [9][10]. The knowledge
domain model encompasses relevant information about the subject
being taught, while the expert model provides feedback based on
students’ responses. The student model represents students’ goals,
needs, and knowledge, adapting in response to the system’s intelli-
gence. The user interface model serves as the bridge, facilitating
seamless interaction between the learner and the system. While
the student model is a crucial element, precisely assessing whether
a student has grasped a concept remains challenging, underscoring
the importance of creating an effective student model within the
system [ 9][10][11][12]. Consequently, this paper would like to
propose an IPT using an effective student model to improve the
efficiency of these systems in studying introductory programming
courses.

This study seeks to answer the questions, “What knowledge
tracing model will best identify and predict the knowledge level
and academic performance of the students?” and “What feedback
provision tool will best give personalized feedback to students based
on their interactions with the system?”. The main aim of the study is
to propose an Intelligent Programming Tutor (IPT) for the learning
enhancement of programming students. Specifically, the objectives
of the study are: To review existing literature related to intelligent
tutoring systems (ITS); To identify a knowledge tracing model that
handles multiple skills given in a programming question, and; To
identify an effective feedback tool for personalized feedback to
students
The scope of this study encompasses an in-depth examination of
Intelligent Programming Tutors (IPTs) and their role in enhancing
the learning experience of students, particularly in the domain of
computer programming. It delves into the architecture of IPTs,
the use of knowledge tracing models for predicting student perfor-
mance, and feedback provision methods. This study draws from a
selection of research papers obtained from reputable online sources
to provide a comprehensive overview of the subject. The study also
focused on knowledge tracing algorithms and models that involve
multiple skills since programming assessments or practice involve
multiple skills.
The study does not include practical implementations or empiri-
cal data from the use of the proposed system. Additionally, while a
comprehensive literature review was conducted to select relevant
studies, the selection process is based on abstracts and titles, which
may not capture all relevant research in the field. Moreover, the
proposed system’s effectiveness and its impact on student learning
outcomes have not been empirically tested. Further research is
needed to validate the practical implications of the proposed sys-
tem and its impact on students’ programming skills and knowledge
acquisition. Lastly, the study is limited to a few research papers
due to time constraints.
2 METHODOLOGY
The research papers used for this study were taken from vari-
ous online sources such as Google Scholar, IEEE Xplore (ieeex-
plore.ieee.org), Research Gate, Cornell University arXiv (arXiv.org),
ACM Digital Library (dl.acm.org), Wiley Online Library, Science
Direct and Springer Link are shown in Table 1. A selection criterion
was used to identify relevant studies that will be used in this study.
A total sixty-one (61) studies were gathered and fourty (40) studies
fit the criteria according to their abstract and title after the filtering
process. Among those, only sixteen (16) was chosen as they were
the most relevant to the study. Keywords such as “Intelligent Tutor-
ing System”, “Intelligent Tutoring System for Programming”, and
“Knowledge tracing models” were typed on the search bar. Then,
the search results were filtered to the years 2018 to 2023, as indi-
cated in Table 2. Studies that were selected were placed in a studies
list and filtered according to their title and abstract, as displayed in
Table 3. Lastly, the studies were chosen according to how well they
can answer the research question of the current study.
3 ANALYSIS, RESULTS AND UNDERSTANDING
The purpose of this literature review is to comprehend the design of
an intelligent tutoring system, recognize the algorithms employed
for knowledge tracing, and the tools used to create personalized
feedback for students based on their inputs, interactions, knowl-
edge level, and other attributes. While prior research presents
various algorithms and models for knowledge tracing and feedback
mechanisms, there are some limitations. First, there is a scarcity of
studies on student models capable of handling the multiple skills
necessary for programming tasks. Existing models struggle when
students need to apply several concepts simultaneously. Second,
the accuracy of these models heavily relies on student data, which
can be challenging in smaller settings. Lastly, while fuzzy logic
and case-based reasoning are used for feedback, there is a need to
explore alternative methods to offer a broader range of personalized
student support.
3.1 INTELLIGENT TUTORING SYSTEM
ARCHITECTURE
Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) are computer-based learning
environments that uses Knowledge Tracing (KT) - algorithms that
helps predict student performance, for designing and scheduling
personalized learning plans for learners [ 3]. Through the different
capabilities of Artificial Intelligence (AI), these systems have con-
tinuously evolved through the combination of various knowledge
gathered from observing the techniques of human tutors [ 13]. The
main aim of these systems is to imitate the role of human tutors in
the life of the students and enhance the learning journey of students
by offering customized learning experiences.

ITSs play a pivotal role in helping students acquire mastery in
specific subject areas, with applications spanning diverse fields, in-
cluding information technology [5]. A study by Mousavinasab et al.
[6] revealed that 37.73% of ITS implementations are concentrated in
the realm of computer science. Given the considerable overlap be-
tween computer science and information technology, ITSs designed
for computer science can be effectively leveraged by information
technology students. Computer programming is a common area
of interest between these two disciplines, and numerous ITSs have
been developed to facilitate computer programming instruction [ 7].
Through these systems, students can engage in focused practice
and skill development, ultimately equipping them with valuable
expertise that benefits their future careers.
The architecture of ITSs is composed of 4 main components: a
student model, a knowledge domain model, a pedagogical or expert
model, and a user interface model [9][10].
The architecture of an Intelligent Tutoring Systems (ITS) in Fig-
ure 1 shows how each of these components interacts with one
another. The knowledge domain or domain model contains rele-
vant knowledge concerning the particular area the ITS is designed
to teach. This may also include the lecture materials prepared for
teaching the topic, such as notes, questions, quizzes, and more. This
model presents the knowledge of experts qualitatively and may
also represent the methods they use to solve problems. This model
evaluates whether the students’ answers are correct [ 9][12]. In
some studies, the expert or pedagogical model presents the domain
model’s contents in a way that satisfies the student’s preference,
generating appropriate feedback to the users based on their re-
sponses. This feedback may determine whether the students are
correct or incorrect, providing the right answers or showing hints
[9][11].
The student model within the system serves as a vital component,
encapsulating each student’s goals, needs, and knowledge. This

3.2 KNOWLEDGE TRACING ALGORITHMS
Knowledge Tracing (KT) is implemented using Artificial Intelli-
gence (AI) algorithms in Intelligent Tutoring Systems to predict the
performance of the students by identifying their current knowledge
states. Several algorithms were used in the selected studies.
Bayesian Network - Bayesian network serves as a model-
ing tool for representing interdependencies among different con-
cepts within a specific domain, utilizing a probability distribution.
Bayesian networks have proven valuable as a probabilistic frame-
work in addressing the dynamic management and continuous up-
dating of student models. It utilizes nodes to represent certain
topics, and is mainly used due to its ability to use probabilities to
deal with uncertainties. This algorithm is used by Eryılmaz and
Adabashi [10].
Neural Network (NN) - Neural networks, due to their capacity
to effectively analyze and identify relationships among multiple
inputs through interconnected nodes, serve as a powerful algorithm
for addressing complex problems and generating a wide range
of outcomes. Neural networks leverage learning techniques that
reduce the complexities associated with handling multiple input
layers, enabling effective decision-making. This algorithm was
utilized by Dutt et al. [11] in conjunction with Fuzzy logic.
Factorization Machines (FM) - Factorization machines (FMs)
are a versatile regression or classification model, and they can be
seen as a unifying framework that encompasses various established
models within the educational literature. They can accurately and
efficiently estimate students’ knowledge and excel even in situations
where student data is lacking. They are also adept at handling
additional contextual information, such as multiple knowledge
components and the number of attempts at the item or skill level.
This is utilized by Vie and Kashima [16], and Choffin et al. [17].
3.3 KNOWLEDGE TRACING MODELS
Since KT is implemented using several algorithms as mentioned in
the previous section, several models have been created to enhance
the effect of the algorithm and to further increase the accuracy of
student performance prediction. These models are used in student
models, and were created based on these algorithms, but the ones
enumerated below are those who focused on the students’ inter-
actions with questions involving multiple skills or concepts since
programming questions involves multiple concepts, especially if
the questions involve writing code [ 14]. DAS3H model combines
the prediction of student performance in practical questions of EKT
and the use of factorization machines using KTM and was chosen
as the model for the proposed sytem.
Exercise-aware Knowledge Tracing (EKT) - proposed by
Liu et al. [ 15] that uses a knowledge state matrix instead of a
Recurrent Neural Network for tracking the state of the learner’s
knowledge in multiple concepts by adding to the knowledge topics
that were already present in each exercise given to the students.
This allows the measurement of how much each exercise can affect
students’ comprehension of various knowledge concepts while they
are exercising.
Knowledge Tracing Machines (KTM) - This was introduced
by Vie and Kashima [ 16] as a family of models that includes es-
tablished models in the EDM literature as specific instances for
addressing knowledge tracing classification problems. The model
allows side information like multiple skills to be included in student
models. Through the use of various datasets, the research reveals
that these machines can accurately estimate user and item param-
eters, even when data is limited. Furthermore, they outperform
existing models, including deep neural networks, in predictive ac-
curacy. KTMs serve as a versatile platform for experimenting with

3.4 FEEDBACK PROVISION TOOLS
Feedback is an essential aspect in Intelligent Programming Tutors
(IPTs) as it gives insight to students on what topics they should
improve on based on their responses to questions. Immediate feed-
back and providing hints are provided by the expert model to help
students progress further in their learning path. Hence, the study
identified two (2) methods which helps the expert model provide
appropriate feedback.
Fuzzy Logic - Fuzzy logic is used in solving the uncertainty that
comes with an e-learning system that adapts to the students’ needs.
This method mirrors human thinking by allowing for gradations
between traditional binary assessments such as yes or no, big or
small, high or low, true or false, offering a more flexible and human-
like decision-making framework. This method is integrated by
Eryılmaz and Adabashi [ 10], Karaci [ 18], and Dutt et al. [ 11] in
their developed ITSs.
Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) - Case-Based Reasoning (CBR)
follows a four-step procedure: initial retrieval of relevant cases, sub-
sequent adaptation of past cases to address the current situation,
potential adjustment of prior solutions, and, if deemed suitable,
preservation of the new case within a case foundation. This ap-
proach allows the system to utilize both historical cases and the
current experiences of students in the creation of tailored solutions.
This method is used by the system to solve a given problem, and
can offer personalized insights and feedback based on the retrieved
past cases. This method was used by Sharma and Harikshan [ 12],
Shweta et al. [19], and Salem [20] in developing an ITS.
4 PROPOSED SYSTEM
In this study, the proposed system is a web-based application de-
signed to enhance students’ mastery of fundamental programming
concepts, skills, and terminology. The application incorporates
various features and techniques to optimize the learning process.
One of the core elements of the web application is the integration
of digital flashcards and diverse study modes. The system incorpo-
rates knowledge tracing, a method for tracking students’ progress
over time. By monitoring their performance and understanding of
the subject matter, the application can tailor content and questions
to each student’s individual learning pace and needs.
This study aims to design an Intelligent Tutoring System (ITS)
based on the DAS3H Model for the Learning Enhancement of Pro-
gramming Students. Specifically, the objectives of the study are as
follows:
• To develop a user-friendly interface for students to access
the course materials.
• To develop quizzes that will assess the students’ learning,
taking into account the time it took to accomplish, the diffi-
culty of the task, and the correctness of their responses.
• To apply the DAS3H model for selecting the appropriate task
difficulty for each student
• To use Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) to provide personalized
feedback on the student’s work
• To evaluate the system by comparing the pretest and posttest
scores of students who used the system and those who used
traditional learning.
Lastly, the DAS3H model, a machine learning algorithm gaining
popularity in educational technology, and Cased-Based Reasoning
(CBR) will be integrated into the application. This model utilizes
multiple factors to predict student performance and offers personal-
ized recommendations based on their learning history. In a recent
study conducted by Choffin et al. [ 17], it was demonstrated that
the DAS3H model significantly amplifies the efficacy of spaced
repetition as a learning strategy, extending its utility beyond con-
ventional memorization tasks to a broader range of educational
contexts, particularly those involving the application of multiple
skills. Sharma and Harikshan [ 12] used CBR as a means to provide
feedback and solutions in an ITS. Using these two techniques will
help provide students with tailored recommendations and tests and
personalized feedback, optimizing their mastery of programming
concepts, skills, and terminology.
The proposed process flow shown in Figure 2 of the current
proposed system based on an existing process flow by Sharma and
Harkishan [12]. The IPT’s primary focus is on aiding students in
crafting more efficient and effective code, emphasizing how the
system manages student responses. It initiates with the student
inputting their code solution to a given problem, which the sys-
tem subsequently evaluates. If the student’s code fails to run, the
system offers feedback through suggestions or hints, allowing the
student to revise their answer. Upon successfully parsing the code,
the system compiles and executes it. The system then compares
the student’s solution with cases stored in the Case-Based Reason-
ing (CBR) Library and provides relevant feedback. If the problem
is solved, it updates the domain and student models, storing in-
puts, performance, and student behavior. Additionally, it saves
the new case in the CBR library for future use. The student and
domain models are updated with information crucial for assessing
the student’s knowledge state, including interactions, feedback, and
performance, and the system selects a new problem adjusted to the
student’s appropriate difficulty level based on the updated student
model.
This new process flow underscores the integral role of Case-
Based Reasoning (CBR) and DAS3H in the system’s functioning,
illustrating the overall system flow following the provision of suit-
able learning materials to the student. The system is expressly
designed to enhance student learning through iterative practice, a

5 IMPLICATION AND CONCLUSION
In conclusion, the proposed Intelligent Programming Tutor (IPT)
represents a significant advancement in the field of programming
education. Based on the chosen studies, DAS3H and Case-Based
Reasoning (CBR) will be utilized to offer a personalized and effective
learning experience to programming students. The proposed system
addresses the challenges associated with programming education,
including high dropout rates, by providing targeted feedback and re-
sources to students. Through DAS3H, the system addresses the crit-
ical issue of memory decay and considers the nuances of multiple
skills, effectively predicting student performance and knowledge
level, making it a valuable tool for learners at various proficiency
levels. The system’s flexible approach to learning materials, includ-
ing spaced repetition, digital flashcards, and diverse study modes,
caters to individual learning preferences.
Additionally, the inclusion of Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) in
the expert model adds an extra layer of sophistication to the feed-
back and guidance provided to students, ensuring that they receive
relevant and tailored assistance. This approach not only improves
programming skills but also enhances problem-solving abilities.
The IPT’s flexible learning materials, adaptability to different learn-
ing styles, and comprehensive knowledge domain model align with
the needs of modern programming students. This tool has the po-
tential to reduce dropout rates and equip learners with the skills and
knowledge needed for success in the continuously evolving field of
technology. The integration of advanced AI techniques and educa-
tional models paves the way for more effective and personalized
programming education.
6 LIMITATION AND FUTURE RESEARCH
The limitations of this study should be considered to guide future
research in the field of Intelligent Programming Tutors (IPTs) and
educational technology. Firstly, this study primarily focuses on the
conceptual design of an IPT with an emphasis on using the DAS3H
model and Case-Based Reasoning (CBR) for feedback provision.
The effectiveness and feasibility of this proposed system need to be
empirically evaluated through extensive testing and user studies
to validate its impact on students’ learning outcomes. In terms of
knowledge tracing models, while the DAS3H model is highlighted,
future research could explore the comparative effectiveness of dif-
ferent knowledge tracing models, including those not covered in
this study, to determine which model best suits the needs of pro-
gramming education. Given the constraints of time, the range of
research papers investigated in this study has been limited. To
facilitate comprehensive insights, future research endeavors should
expand their scope by exploring a wider array of online databases
and incorporating additional studies. This approach would enable
a more thorough examination of diverse possibilities and avenues
for investigation. Lastly, further development is needed for this
study since it only included models.
