Contemporary knowledge tracing models have made substantial progress in modeling cognitive learning pat-
terns, yet their predominant focus on performance metrics overlooks the profound inﬂuence of aﬀective states 
on educational processes. Aﬀective factors critically shape students’ attentional engagement, concept assimila-
tion, and problem-solving consistency – dynamics that existing cognitive-centric frameworks often fail to cap-
ture. We propose the Aﬀective Hypergraph Knowledge Tracing (AHGKT) framework, which incorporates aﬀective 
intelligence into knowledge tracing through a dual-layer hypergraph architecture. The exercise layer establishes 
high-order relationships among questions and knowledge concepts, while the aﬀection layer tracks aﬀective pat-
terns throughout learning sessions. A novel hypergraph convolution operator synergistically fuses these cognitive 
and aﬀective signals, enabling simultaneous modeling of concept mastery and aﬀective impacts through hier-
archical graph propagation. Extensive experiments on three real-world datasets demonstrate the superiority of 
AHGKT, achieving statistically signiﬁcant accuracy improvements of up to 5% over state-of-the-art baselines. The 
framework’s eﬀectiveness is further validated through ablation studies quantifying the complementary beneﬁts 
of hypergraph modeling and aﬀective feature integration. By modeling aﬀection as a quantiﬁable predictor of 
learning outcomes and introducing hypergraph neural networks into the educational domain, this work oﬀers 
a promising methodological contribution toward aﬀect-aware intelligent tutoring systems. Our ﬁndings provide 
practical insights and design principles for developing educational AI that better captures the interplay between 
cognitive and aﬀective dimensions in learning.
1.  Introduction
With the development of educational data mining, Knowledge Trac-
ing has become a key direction in intelligent education research. It aims 
to assess students’ knowledge mastery and predict their learning per-
formance (Abdelrahman et al., 2023; Tomasevic et al., 2020). Accurate 
modeling of students’ knowledge states not only enhances learning eﬀec-
tiveness but also supports intelligent services such as student proﬁling 
(Zong et al., 2020)and personalized resource recommendation (Wang 
et al., 2021).
The evolution of KT methods has seen signiﬁcant advancements.

challenges for constructing aﬀective knowledge tracing models, specif-
ically: (1) how to accurately represent aﬀective states, and (2) how to 
characterize the complex interplay between aﬀective states and under-
lying knowledge concepts.
To address these challenges, we construct an exercise-aﬀection hier-
archical hypergraph and propose an aﬀect-aware hypergraph convolu-
tion mechanism to explicitly capture high-order relationships among ex-
ercises involving the same concept (e.g., 𝑞2, 𝑞3, and 𝑞5 for 𝑐3). This novel 
method, named Aﬀective Hypergraph Knowledge Tracing (AHGKT), in-
tegrates aﬀective states and exercise-concept interactions to enhance 
the representation of student knowledge states. AHGKT also reveals the 
inﬂuence of aﬀective ﬂuctuations on learning behaviors, supporting per-
sonalized learning and real-time intervention strategies. The key contri-
butions of this work are summarized as follows.
• We incorporate aﬀective factors into the knowledge tracing frame-
work, enhancing the precision and adaptability of knowledge state 
representation by integrating intrinsic factors such as individual stu-
dent diﬀerences and problem diﬃculty.
• We propose an aﬀect-aware hypergraph convolution mechanism, 
which integrates representations from the exercise and aﬀection lay-
ers in a hierarchical hypergraph to capture complex high-order inter-
actions among exercises, knowledge concepts, and aﬀective states.
• To handle both supervised and unsupervised scenarios, we introduce 
specialized optimization strategies, enabling the model to learn the 
inﬂuence of aﬀective states on learning behaviors even in the ab-
sence of labeled aﬀective data.
• Experimental results on multiple datasets demonstrate that our 
model outperforms traditional methods in terms of both accuracy 
and stability.

2.  Related work
In this section, we review the work related to knowledge tracing.
2.1.  Knowledge tracing
Traditional knowledge tracing models, such as BKT (Corbett & An-
derson, 1994), use Hidden Markov Models (Baker et al., 2008) to dynam-
ically track students’ knowledge states. Several important extensions of 
BKT include personalized BKT, which introduces individual parameters 
to capture student-speciﬁc diﬀerences (Lee & Brunskill, 2012; Yudelson 
et al., 2013). For instance, Pardos and Heﬀernan (Pardos & Heﬀernan, 
2010) focused on heuristically personalizing the prior knowledge proba-
bility 𝑝 for each student across diﬀerent skills. Additionally, Käser et al. 
(2017) proposed using a Dynamic Bayesian Network to jointly model 
multiple skills and the dependencies between them, aiming to capture 
hierarchical relationships among skills within a single model.
A pioneering deep learning approach in knowledge tracing is the 
DKT model (Piech et al., 2015) , which leverages RNNs (Lipton et al., 
2015) to predict the probability of a student answering a question cor-
rectly at each time step. Some extended DKT models improve perfor-
mance by incorporating problem features like problem ID (Sonkar et al., 
2020) and known diﬃculty  (Shen et al., 2022), or student-related factors 
such as response rate and practice time (Xiong et al., 2016). DKT+ (Ye-
ung & Yeung, 2018) reﬁnes the loss function of the original DKT model 
by incorporating two additional regularization terms. DKVMN (Zhang 
et al., 2017) employed a combination of static key matrices and dy-
namic value matrices, further enhancing DKT’s ability to track the state 
of each concept over time. XKT (Huang et al., 2024b) model integrates 
multidimensional item response theory into knowledge tracing, simu-
lating students’ selective perception and knowledge memory processes 
to enhance interpretability. THKT (Sun et al., 2024) dynamically tracks

In the task of knowledge tracing, the introduction of attention mech-
anisms addresses the limitation of traditional models that treat all exer-
cises equally. Attention mechanisms can identify the importance of dif-
ferent exercises when predicting the target question and weight various 
interactions accordingly, thereby improving prediction accuracy. SAKT 
(Pandey & Karypis, 2019) is the ﬁrst model to introduce self-attention 
mechanisms into knowledge tracing. It uses the scaled dot-product at-
tention mechanism and multiple attention heads to learn the importance 
of diﬀerent exercises during prediction. AKT (Ghosh et al., 2020) in-
troduces a mechanism called "monotonic attention," which allows the 
attention weights to decay exponentially over time, reﬂecting the tem-
poral relevance of student interactions. SAINT (Choi et al., 2020) further 
extends the Transformer architecture by separating question embed-
dings and student response embeddings, and incorporates time-related 
features, such as response time, to reﬁne the attention weights and cap-
ture the temporal impact on the learning process.
However, most of these methods consider diﬀerent exercises, elapsed 
time and other factors, while ignoring the importance of students’ emo-
tional state to the prediction results. By introducing emotional factors 
into the attention mechanism, the model can adjust the attention to dif-
ferent exercises and thus improve the prediction accuracy.
2.2.  Graph-based knowledge tracing
Compared to traditional knowledge tracing methods, graph-based 
models excel at capturing the complex relationships between students, 
problems, and skills, enabling more comprehensive learning state pre-
dictions. The GKT (Nakagawa et al., 2019) model represents knowledge 
components (KCs) and their dependencies as a graph structure, trans-
forming the knowledge tracing task into a time-series node-level classi-
ﬁcation problem. The PEBG (Liu et al., 2020) model employs a bipartite 
graph to embed problems and skills, integrating exercise diﬃculty  in-
formation during training to enhance prediction accuracy. SGKT (Wu 
et al., 2022) employs gated graph neural networks to capture students’ 
knowledge states from session graphs and utilizes GNN (Scarselli et al., 
2008) to derive embeddings for exercises and skills in the relationship 
graph.
To further alleviate representation sparsity, Bi-CLKT (Song et al., 
2022) introduced contrastive learning on question-KC and KC-KC graphs 
to produce question embeddings enhanced with structural information 
from both questions and KCs. More recently, TCL4KT (Sun et al., 2023) 
introduced a weighted heterogeneous graph-based contrastive learning 
framework to enhance question representation learning for knowledge 
tracing. Beyond pairwise graph modeling, the DGEKT (Cui et al., 2024) 
model leverages a conceptual association hypergraph (Chen et al., 2020; 
Feng et al., 2019) to model related knowledge concepts within learning 
interactions, eﬀectively capturing higher-order relationships among ex-
ercise concepts.
However, most existing graph-based KT models primarily focus on 
structural representation learning and neglect the dynamic role of learn-
ers’ emotional states during the learning process. By introducing an af-
fective attention mechanism into the hypergraph framework, emotional 
states can be propagated among skill-related exercises through hyper-
graph convolution, enabling precise tracking of students’ personalized 
knowledge states.
2.3.  Aﬀective eﬀects
Craig et al. (2004) investigated the emotional changes experienced 
by college students while learning the basics of computers. Utilizing ob-
servational methods and a self-designed scale analysis, it was found that 
confusion and ﬂuency during the learning process are signiﬁcantly pos-
itively correlated with learning outcomes, whereas boredom exhibits 
a signiﬁcant negative correlation. Pedro et al. (2013) employed a lo-
gistic regression model to analyze data from the ASSISTment system, 
demonstrating that lower levels of boredom, higher levels of confusion, 
and reduced instances of careless errors are signiﬁcantly associated with 
an increased probability of college admission. Pardos et al. (2013) ex-
amined the distribution of emotional states among students with diﬀer-
ent learning outcomes, ﬁnding that successful students typically demon-
strate balanced focus and moderate frustration, while unsuccessful stu-
dents are more prone to boredom or gaming behaviors. Liu et al. (2022) 
developed a BERT-CNN-based model to automatically detect emotional 
and cognitive engagement in MOOC discussions and demonstrated their 
joint eﬀects on learning achievement. Furthermore, Wang et al. (2024) 
integrated emotion-based estimates of guess and slip parameters with 
the fundamental neural cognitive diagnostic framework derived from 
the DINA model to diagnose the knowledge proﬁciency levels of stu-
dents.
However, despite the revealed association between emotional states 
and learning performance, how to incorporate emotional dynamics into 
knowledge tracing models to enhance the precision of modeling stu-
dents’ knowledge states remains a key challenge.

4.1.  Exercise-aﬀection hierarchical hypergraph
The hierarchical hypergraph consists of the exercise layer and the af-
fection layer. The exercise layer connects exercise nodes through hyper-
edges based on knowledge concepts, capturing the high-dimensional re-
lationships between exercises and knowledge points. The aﬀection layer 
models the interactions between students’ aﬀection states and exercises, 
capturing the inﬂuence of aﬀection on learning behaviors.

associations between aﬀection nodes and other elements. This integra-
tion enables a more comprehensive and individualized representation 
of students’ emotional states.

In this section, we conducted extensive experiments and provided

• RQ1: Does AHGKT improve knowledge state prediction performance

• RQ2: How do diﬀerent components of the AHGKT model impact the

5.4.  Ablation study (RQ2)
In this section, we will address Q2 by conducting ablation experi-
ments on the model. First, we validate the eﬀectiveness of the aﬀective 
interaction and hypergraph structure on three datasets. Then, we ex-

5.4.1.  Component adjustment
As shown in Table 5, it is evident that both the aﬀective module and 
the hypergraph structure play critical roles in enhancing the predictive 
performance of the knowledge tracing model. Firstly, the model with-
out the aﬀective module (AHGKT-NF) shows a noticeable decrease in 
both AUC and ACC across all datasets, with signiﬁcant drops observed 
in ASSIST2009 and ASSIST2012, where the AUC scores fell to 0.7326 
and 0.7281, respectively. This indicates that the aﬀective module ef-
fectively captures changes in students’ aﬀective states, contributing to 
more accurate predictions of knowledge mastery. Secondly, replacing 
the hypergraph with an ordinary graph (AHGKT-OG) results in a sub-
stantial performance decline on the ASSIST2017 dataset, with the AUC 
dropping to 0.7324. Here, the ordinary graph refers to a conventional 
pairwise graph structure, where each edge connects only two nodes. The 
hypergraph structure excels at capturing the complex relationships be-
tween questions and skills, enabling the model to better represent the 
dependencies among knowledge points. These results demonstrate that 
the inclusion of both the aﬀective module and the hypergraph structure 
signiﬁcantly enhances AHGKT’s performance, making it well-suited for 
knowledge tracing tasks.

6.  Conclusion
We present an aﬀective hypergraph knowledge tracing (AHGKT), a 
knowledge tracing framework that innovatively integrates aﬀective dy-
namics through a bilevel hypergraph architecture. The exercise layer 
models high-order question-concept relationships, while the aﬀective 
layer tracks emotion-driven behavioral patterns. Our aﬀect-aware hy-
pergraph convolution mechanism fuses these cognitive and emotional 
signals via multilayer message passing, enabling precise knowledge state 
modeling. Evaluations across three datasets demonstrate AHGKT’s supe-
riority and cross-domain robustness. By unifying hypergraph learning 
with aﬀective computing, this work advances educational AI through 
two critical insights: aﬀective states are quantiﬁable predictors of learn-
ing outcomes, and hypergraphs inherently capture the multidimensional 
interactions in learning systems. The framework provides a blueprint for 
emotionally intelligent tutoring tools that address both cognitive and af-
fective dimensions of education.
In future work, we will explore rapid adaptation mechanisms (e.g., 
meta-learning) to improve modeling for new users, and investigate 
cross-domain representation learning to enhance transferability across 
courses and platforms. We also plan to incorporate richer behavioral sig-
nals and adopt a causal perspective to better understand the dynamic 
interplay between aﬀect and knowledge acquisition, thereby improving 
the robustness and interpretability of aﬀect-aware knowledge tracing 
systems.
