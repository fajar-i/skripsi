Abstract—Math difficulties, such as dyscalculia, can hinder
children’s ability to acquire strong mathematical problem-solving
capabilities, limiting their potential for success. Early identifica-
tion and intervention are essential in addressing these challenges.
This research paper introduces EDSense, a specialized Bayesian
Knowledge Tracing (BKT) model developed for math skill assess-
ment in children who may have dyscalculia. The system employs
a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) to represent the dependencies
between probabilities and incorporates the prior probability of
mastery, slip rate, and guessing rate to update the student’s
knowledge state. The case study findings revealed challenges in
math comprehension, multiplication, and symbol identification
among children who may have dyscalculia, highlighting the
need for targeted interventions and instructional strategies. The
personalized and data-driven approach of EDSense contributes
to the field of dyscalculia intervention, providing a promising tool
for math skill assessment in children who may have dyscalculia.
The development of EDSense has the potential to enhance early
identification and intervention for children with dyscalculia,
leading to improved math outcomes and better support for
students with this specific learning disorder.
Index Terms —Bayesian Knowledge Tracing, dyscalculia, per-
sonalized learning
I. I NTRODUCTION
In today’s society, the importance of mathematical problem-
solving skills cannot be overstated. According to the Na-
tional Research Council (2001), these skills are crucial for
success in academia and are vital in various fields, including
science, engineering, and technology. However, some chil-
dren face challenges in developing strong math skills due
to various factors. For example, insufficient number sense,
which refers to the foundational understanding of numbers
and their relationships, can hinder children’s ability to grasp
mathematical concepts and perform calculations accurately [1]
[2]. Additionally, neurobiological disorders such as dyscalculia
or dyslexia can also impact the development of math skills
[3]Furthermore, ineffective teaching methods that do not cater
to diverse learning needs can further hinder the development
of math problem-solving capabilities in children [4].
To address these challenges, the ’EDSense’ Research initia-
tive has been developed as an innovative educational interven-
tion to improve the mathematical problem-solving capabilities
of children above the age of 6-10 years. The initiative employs
a dynamic intervention approach using a Dynamic Bayesian
Network where Directed Acyclic Graphs (DAGs) are used
for math skill topologies based on the difficulty level of the
skills. By providing personalized feedback and tailored learn-
ing experiences that cater to individual students’ needs, the
initiative aims to enhance their problem-solving capabilities
[5]. Moreover, the initiative adopts a collaborative approach
involving educators, parents, and educational institutions to
create a supportive learning environment [2].
This work aims to explore the effectiveness of the ’ED-
Sense’ initiative in improving the mathematical problem-
solving capabilities of children. Previous studies have shown
that targeted interventions that cater to individual learning
needs can significantly improve math skills [6]. Therefore, this
paper will evaluate the effectiveness of the ’EDSense’ initia-
tive in addressing math difficulties such as insufficient num-
ber sense, neurobiological disorders, and ineffective teaching
methods. The paper will also discuss the potential implications
of this research for educational institutions, educators, and
parents, and highlight the significance of early intervention

II. R ELATED WORK
Several studies have been conducted to investigate the
mathematical abilities and challenges faced by students with
dyscalculia, as well as interventions aimed at improving early
number sense skills in preschool and kindergarten children.
Jeya, S., Albina, A. (2019) conducted a study titled ”A Study
of Students with Dyscalculia and their Mathematical Abilities
at Primary Schools in Karaikudi” in Tamil Nadu, India, which
found that many students in the Karaikudi region struggled
with mathematical concepts and a significant number of them
had dyscalculia [7]. The study utilized a survey, diagnostic
testing, and comparison of the mathematical abilities of stu-
dents with and without dyscalculia as a research methodology.
Qi Liu et al. (2019) proposed in a research paper titled
”Exploiting Cognitive Structure for Adaptive Learning” which
explored the use of the cognitive structure as a tool for
creating adaptive learning systems. The authors argued that
understanding a learner’s cognitive structure, knowledge, and
understanding of a given subject, can help tailor instruction
to suit individual needs. The paper presented a theoretical
framework and a prototype system incorporating cognitive
structure for adaptive learning, suggesting potential for sig-
nificant improvement in adaptive learning systems [8]
Lijin Zhang et al. (2016) focused on early screening and
dynamic intervention for children with insufficient number
sense, aiming to improve math-solving capabilities in children
above 10 years of age who face difficulties with math. The re-
search aimed to enhance number sense, the ability to read and
understand numbers and perform mathematical calculations,
particularly in children with dyscalculia [9].
There have also been several studies examining effective
teaching strategies and interventions for developing early num-
ber sense skills in preschool and kindergarten children. For
example, Clements and Sarama [10] provided a comprehensive
review of the literature on early mathematics education and
discussed the importance of developing early number sense

presented a conceptual framework for a professional develop-

Vandermaas-Peeler et al. [12] conducted a systematic review of
intervention studies aimed at developing early number sense
in preschool children, providing insights into various inter-
vention strategies and recommendations for future research.
Various studies have examined the importance of number

III. P ROPOSED METHODOLOGY
A total of 137 participants aged 6-10 years, who had access
to a personal computer (PC) and an internet connection,
were recruited from local schools in Mumbai to participate
in this case study. The Smartick online math learning plat-
form [33] was utilized as the assessment tool in this study.
It offers personalized math lessons and exercises and em-
ploys adaptive technology to customize the math curriculum
according to the individualized level and pace of learning
of each child. The platform provides an engaging audio-
visual interface for interactive assessments, covering primary
math skills such as Dot Comparison, Subitizing, Number
Comparison, Mental Number Line, Number Line, Counting,
Addition, Subtraction, and Multiplication. Data collected from
the Smartick assessments, including performance measures
such as accuracy, speed, and completion time, were recorded
and analyzed using descriptive statistics. This analysis allowed
for the identification of patterns or trends in the children’s
math skills. The data were also used in selecting samples
for testing the proposed prototype. Following analysis of
Smartick assessment results and observations, an EDSense
prototype was designed for the proposed intervention. The
prototype was tested with dyscalculia specialists, including
math educators, psychologists, and researchers. Expert feed-
back was incorporated into refining the prototype to align with
best practices and current research in dyscalculia intervention.
EDSense is a specialized Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT)
model designed to analyze assessment data, including accurate
answers, incorrect answers, and specific types of incorrect

answers commonly made by students with dyscalculia. The
model is tailored to track and model students’ knowledge
and performance, taking into account the specific mistakes
they may make related to addition, multiplication, carrying
digits, vanishing digits, individual digit addition, and the ”zero
makes zero” concept. This allows for personalized feedback
and support to address the unique learning needs of students
with dyscalculia.
The Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) model used in
EDSense updates the student’s knowledge state based on the
probability of mastery, which is inferred from the student’s
responses to questions. The model uses a directed acyclic
graph (DAG) to represent the dependencies between probabil-
ities. For skills like Addition, Subtraction, and Multiplication,
separate DAGs are created. The DAG for Addition and Sub-
traction includes 16 micro levels, ranging from simple 1-digit
addition to complex 3-digit addition with carry. The observable
nodes in the DAG are the student’s responses, while the hidden
nodes represent the student’s inferred knowledge states. The
DAG starts with the prior probability of mastery, which is
updated using learning rate and guessing rate. The slip rate is
also updated, and finally, the posterior probability is computed
based on the updated prior probability and slip rate. Bayesian
Knowledge Tracing (BKT) is a statistical method used in
educational data mining that models a student’s knowledge
or skill state while interacting with an educational system
using Bayesian probabilities. The BKT model consists of three
probabilities: The prior probability of mastery (P(m)): This
represents the probability that the student has already mastered
the skill before interacting with the system. It is typically set
to a low value, such as 0.1, to reflect that most students do
not start with full mastery [34].
Prior probability of mastery (P(m)) based on correct an-
swers:
P (m) = P (m) + (1 − P (m)) ∗ P (l) (1)
Slip rate (P(s)) based on specific wrong answers:
P (s) = P (s) + (1 − P (s)) ∗ P (g) (2)
Posterior probability of mastery (P(p)) based on correct an-
swers:
P (p) = P (m)/(P (m) + P (s)) (3)
Where P(m) represents the prior probability of mastery,
P(l) represents the learning rate, P(s) represents the slip rate,
and P(g) represents the guessing rate. These equations can
be used to update the probabilities in the BKT model for
analyzing student performance data in the context of math
skill assessment.

IV. R ESULT AND DISCUSSIONS
When comparing the written and audio-visual assessments
for dyscalculia, several key factors come into play. The
written assessment predominantly relies on reading compre-
hension and requires the child to translate problems into
written formats, perform mental calculations, and provide
written solutions. In contrast, the audio-visual assessment
combines auditory and visual modalities, presenting instruc-
tions verbally while displaying relevant information on the
screen. This approach allows the child to process information
through both modalities simultaneously and refer back to
visual cues during calculations. While the written assessment
places higher demands on reading and written skills, the
audio-visual assessment offers a more immediate response
and engages multiple senses, potentially catering to different
learning strengths and preferences. Considering the specific
needs and abilities of each child is crucial in selecting the
most appropriate assessment method or utilizing a combination
to provide a comprehensive evaluation of dyscalculia. The
audio-visual Smartick tool, with its combination of auditory
instructions and visual cues, is an ideal choice for dyscalculia
assessment due to its ability to engage multiple senses and
provide immediate feedback. Therefore, Smartick is selected
as the preferred tool for assessing dyscalculia.

V. C ONCLUSION
In conclusion, this research paper presents the develop-
ment of EDSense, a specialized Bayesian Knowledge Tracing
(BKT) model for math skill assessment in children with
dyscalculia. Through the assessment of 137 participants aged
6-10 years using the Smartick online math learning platform,
data on accuracy, speed, and completion time were collected
and analyzed using descriptive statistics. Based on the analysis,
an EDSense prototype was designed and tested with experts
in dyscalculia.
EDSense utilizes a directed acyclic graph (DAG) to repre-
sent the dependencies between probabilities and incorporates
prior probability of mastery, slip rate, and guessing rate to
update the student’s knowledge state. The system also includes
a question selection module based on student performance,
including correct answers, wrong answers, and specific wrong
answers that indicate common mistakes made by students with
dyscalculia.
The findings from the case study highlight challenges in
math comprehension, multiplication, and symbol identification
among children with dyscalculia, underscoring the need for
targeted interventions and instructional strategies. EDSense,
with its personalized and data-driven approach, presents a
promising tool for math skill assessment in children with
dyscalculia, contributing to the field of dyscalculia interven-
tion.
Further research and development of EDSense could have
significant implications for identifying and addressing math
difficulties in children with dyscalculia, ultimately improving
their math skills and academic outcomes. EDSense has the
potential to be integrated into educational settings as a valuable
tool for educators and practitioners working with children with
dyscalculia, aiding in the development of effective interven-
tions and instructional strategies tailored to the unique needs
of these students.
